The ability to teach, whether at the undergraduate or graduate level, is an essential skill for physicists, enabling them to communicate scientific ideas effectively. During my time at the University of Florida, I have served as both a Teaching Assistant and an Instructor for undergraduate courses, most notably Physics II Without Calculus. My teaching efforts have been recognized with two awards:: the [GSTA Award (2024)]{https://successful-teaching.com/2024/04/26/uf-graduate-student-teaching-award-winners-2023-2024/} and the [Charles F. Hooper Jr. Award (2025)]{https://phys.ufl.edu/2025/05/27/faculty-staff-and-graduate-student-awards-for-2024/}.

My teaching philosophy is captured by a quote often attributed to Einstein: “If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough.” I firmly believe that physics should be accessible to everyone. Accordingly, I strive to communicate physical concepts in a way that emphasizes an intuitive understanding of the fundamental principles underlying a phenomenon, rather than focusing primarily on the mathematical formalism used to describe it. My goal is to help students develop a genuine understanding of the physics, enabling them to appreciate not only how a result is derived, but also why it holds.
